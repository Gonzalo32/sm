## 1. Executive Objective & Architectural Findings

Checkpoint **CP33.3.x** performed an empirical, reproducible forensic investigation to answer the core architectural question:

> **Can our extension obtain sufficient historical market data directly from TradeSea's native chart/series interface through normal platform interactions (timeframe switching, historical scroll, zoom)?**

### Architectural Conclusion:
* **`YES (For Single-Session Live Operation)`**: The Chrome Extension (`pageBridge.ts`) successfully intercepts TradeSea's WebSocket feed (`wss://app.tradesea.ai/ws`) and receives 31 real candles on initialization and on every timeframe change. This is 100% sufficient for live intraday monitoring, single-session ICT structure detection, HUD overlay rendering, and canvas drawing primitive generation.
* **`PARTIAL / LIMITED (For Multi-Month Backtesting)`**: Intraday timeframes (1m, 5m, 15m) are strictly bounded by TradeSea's 31-candle initial push limit (2.5 hours on 5m). Scrolling left does **NOT** trigger additional WebSocket data downloads. Therefore, multi-month continuous historical backtesting cannot rely solely on native TradeSea chart scrolling.

## 4. Direct Answers to Required Questions (Q1 – Q10)

### Q1: Does TradeSea deliver additional history upon normal chart interaction?
**Answer**: `YES (Via Timeframe Switching)`. Changing timeframe (e.g. 5m $\rightarrow$ 15m $\rightarrow$ 1H $\rightarrow$ 1D) unsubscribes the active series and receives a new 31-candle historical series tailored to the selected timeframe.

### Q2: Is historical data loaded into the client series even if not currently visible?
**Answer**: `YES`. The WebSocket `timescale_update` message loads 31 candles into memory, even if only 10–15 candles fit inside the active visual viewport.

### Q3: Does scrolling left trigger new data acquisition?
**Answer**: `NO`. Mover el gráfico hacia la izquierda shifts the visual canvas offset over the existing loaded series in memory. Zero WebSocket frame requests or responses are emitted.

### Q4: Does changing timeframe generate a new series?
**Answer**: `YES`. Timeframe switching sends a timeframe subscription frame, and TradeSea responds with a fresh 31-bar `timescale_update` block for that timeframe.

### Q5: Do `timescale_update` messages contain historical data as well as realtime updates?
**Answer**: `YES`. The initial `timescale_update` frame contains an array `s` holding 31 historical bars. Subsequent frames deliver real-time candle updates.

### Q6: What is the maximum observed depth per timeframe?
**Answer**:
* 1m: 31 candles (31 minutes)
* 5m: 31 candles (2.5 hours)
* 15m: 31 candles (7.75 hours)
* 1H: 31 candles (31 hours)
* 4H: 31 candles (124 hours)
* 1D: 31 candles (31 days)

### Q7: Are 60 days reached?
**Answer**: `NO`. On intraday timeframes (1m, 5m, 15m), max depth is 31 candles (2.5 hours on 5m). On 1D, 31 candles span 31 days (~1 month).

### Q8: What is the REAL maximum observed depth?
**Answer**: Intraday (5m) = 31 candles / 2.5 hours (`1779128100000` $\rightarrow$ `1779137100000`). Daily (1D) = 31 candles / 31 days.

### Q9: Can the extension reasonably use the TradeSea chart as a runtime source?
**Answer**: `YES_FOR_SINGLE_SESSION`. For live real-time analysis, HUD state rendering, and single-session intraday ICT detection, TradeSea's native WebSocket stream is fully viable.

### Q10: What limitations remain for CP33.4?
**Answer**: Multi-session continuous historical data for deep out-of-sample backtesting remains bounded by TradeSea's 31-bar initial push limit on intraday timeframes.
